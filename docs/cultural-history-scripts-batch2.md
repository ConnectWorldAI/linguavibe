When you learn French, you're learning what happens when Germanic warriors try to speak Latin for 1,500 years. History shaped the language. Now learn both. ConnectWorld AI."

When you learn Japanese, you're not just learning words — you're learning a 500-year-old social code. Want to learn more of the culture? ConnectWorld AI — where the greatest scholars learn."

### Script 23: "Why Japanese Has Three Writing Systems (And You Need All of Them)"

Soft natural light through paper screens. 9:16 vertical. Documentary film quality.

**Narration script:**
"Open any Japanese newspaper and you'll see three completely different writing systems used in the same sentence. Kanji — Chinese characters imported 1,500 years ago — for meaning-heavy words. When Japan imported Chinese writing around 500 CE, it didn't fit Japanese grammar at all. Chinese is isolating — no conjugations, no particles. Japanese is agglutinative — it stacks suffixes and particles endlessly. So the Japanese invented hiragana to handle the grammar that kanji couldn't express. Katakana was created by Buddhist monks as shorthand for reading Chinese texts aloud. Here's the fascinating part: hiragana was originally called 'women's writing' because noble women in the Heian court used it to write literature — including The Tale of Genji, the world's first novel, written by Lady Murasaki in 1008 CE. Women's writing became the foundation of Japanese literature. The culture runs deep. Learn the language that carries it. ConnectWorld AI."

**Text overlays:** "3 scripts: Kanji + Hiragana + Katakana" | "Hiragana = 'women's writing' → became literary standard" | "Tale of Genji (1008 CE) = world's first novel"
