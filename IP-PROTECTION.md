# Fluence — Intellectual Property Protection & Legal Playbook

## Why This Matters

You're building something that doesn't exist yet. That means you need to lock it down BEFORE someone sees it and tries to copy it. The moment you go public — App Store listing, social media, pitch deck — people will see your features and try to replicate them. Here's exactly how to protect yourself.

---

## Step-by-Step Protection Plan

### STEP 1: Trademark Your Brand (Do This FIRST — Week 1)

**What to trademark:**
- "Fluence" (word mark)
- "Fluence" + logo (the geometric F icon) (design mark)
- "Fluence World" (if used as a separate product name)
- "Watch & Learn" (if used as a feature name)
- "Dream Vacation Mode" (if used as a feature name)
- Tagline: whatever your launch tagline is

**How to file:**
1. Go to [USPTO.gov](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks) (US Patent & Trademark Office)
2. Use TEAS Plus filing ($250 per class) or TEAS Standard ($350 per class)
3. File under **Class 9** (mobile applications, software) and **Class 41** (education, entertainment services)
4. You need BOTH classes — that's $500-$700 total for basic filing

**Before filing, do a search:**
1. Search [TESS (Trademark Electronic Search System)](https://tess2.uspto.gov/)
2. Search "Fluence" in Class 9 and Class 41
3. If someone has it in a different class (like perfume), you're fine
4. If it's taken in software/education, you may need to modify (e.g., "Fluence AI" or "Fluence Language")

**Timeline:** 8-12 months for approval, but you have protection from filing date
**Cost:** $250-$700 DIY, or $1,500-$3,000 with a trademark attorney (recommended)

**International trademarks:**
- File a Madrid Protocol application through USPTO for international coverage
- Priority countries: EU (EUIPO), UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, Mexico
- Cost: $2,000-$5,000 for major markets
- Do this within 6 months of US filing to claim priority date

### STEP 2: Copyright Registration (Week 1-2)

**What to copyright:**
- All source code (the entire codebase)
- UI/UX designs (screen layouts, visual design)
- Written content (lesson scripts, teacher dialogues, marketing copy)
- The app icon and all visual assets
- Strategy documents, business plans
- Any original music or audio you create
- Video content generated by the platform

**How to file:**
1. Go to [Copyright.gov](https://www.copyright.gov/registration/)
2. Register as "Computer Program" (for code) — $65 per registration
3. Register visual works separately — $65 per registration
4. Register literary works (scripts, content) — $65 per registration

**Important:** Copyright exists automatically when you create something, but REGISTRATION gives you:
- Ability to sue for statutory damages ($30,000-$150,000 per infringement)
- Presumption of ownership in court
- Ability to get attorney's fees if you win

**Cost:** $195-$400 total for basic registrations
**Timeline:** 3-6 months for certificate, but protection from filing date

### STEP 3: Provisional Patent Application (Week 2-4)

**What's patentable in Fluence:**

| Innovation | Patent Type | Why It's Patentable |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Song translation engine that preserves rhythm/beat while translating lyrics | Utility Patent | Novel method of rhythm-constrained machine translation |
| AI teacher system with persistent memory and personality adaptation | Utility Patent | Novel architecture for personalized AI tutoring |
| City-level dialect teaching system with real-time slang updates | Utility Patent | Novel method of geo-tagged linguistic data aggregation |
| Voice cloning for language learning (hear yourself in another language) | Utility Patent | Novel application of voice synthesis for education |
| Battle Mode (real-time competitive language assessment) | Utility Patent | Novel gamification method for language proficiency testing |
| Virtual classroom with AI teacher + multiple real students | Utility Patent | Novel hybrid AI-human group learning architecture |
| Music-based vocabulary extraction and lesson generation | Utility Patent | Novel method of automated curriculum generation from audio |
| Social media slang detection → lesson pipeline | Utility Patent | Novel method of real-time educational content generation |

**How to file a Provisional Patent Application (PPA):**
1. Write a detailed description of each invention (how it works, what makes it new)
2. Include diagrams, flowcharts, system architecture
3. File at [USPTO.gov](https://www.uspto.gov/patents) — $320 for small entity
4. This gives you "Patent Pending" status for 12 months
5. Within 12 months, file the full non-provisional patent ($1,600-$3,000+)

**Why provisional first:**
- Cheap ($320 vs. $3,000+)
- Establishes your priority date (proves you invented it first)
- Gives you 12 months to raise money/validate before spending on full patent
- You can say "Patent Pending" on everything

**Cost:** $320 DIY, or $2,000-$5,000 with a patent attorney (HIGHLY recommended for software patents)
**Timeline:** Immediate "Patent Pending" status upon filing

### STEP 4: Trade Secrets Protection (Ongoing)

**What qualifies as a trade secret:**
- Your AI prompt engineering (how you make teachers sound natural)
- Your slang verification pipeline (how you validate new slang)
- Your recommendation algorithms (how you match songs/teachers to users)
- Your voice synthesis parameters (how you make voice cloning sound real)
- Your data sources and scraping methods
- Your business strategy and revenue projections
- Your teacher personality design system

**How to protect trade secrets:**

1. **NDAs (Non-Disclosure Agreements)** — EVERYONE signs one before seeing anything:
   - Co-founders
   - Employees
   - Contractors/freelancers
   - Investors (yes, even investors)
   - Beta testers (simplified version)
   - Partners/vendors

2. **Employment/Contractor agreements must include:**
   - Non-disclosure clause
   - Non-compete clause (where enforceable — check your state)
   - Work-for-hire clause (everything they create belongs to the company)
   - Invention assignment clause (any related inventions belong to company)
   - Non-solicitation clause (can't poach your team/clients)

3. **Technical protection:**
   - Never put proprietary algorithms in public repos
   - Use private GitHub/GitLab repositories
   - Limit access (not everyone needs to see everything)
   - Encrypt sensitive documents
   - Use access logs (know who viewed what, when)
   - Watermark confidential documents

4. **Document everything:**
   - Keep dated records of when you created each feature
   - Save all design documents, sketches, notes
   - Use version control (git) with timestamps
   - Email yourself descriptions of inventions (poor man's patent — creates dated record)

### STEP 5: Business Entity Formation (Week 1)

**Form an LLC or Corporation IMMEDIATELY:**

**Why:**
- Separates personal assets from business liability
- Required for trademark/patent ownership
- Required for investor funding
- Tax advantages
- Professional credibility

**Recommended structure:**
- **Delaware C-Corp** if you plan to raise VC funding (investors prefer this)
- **LLC** if you're bootstrapping initially (simpler, cheaper, flexible)
- You can convert LLC → C-Corp later when raising

**How to form:**
1. Use [Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas) ($500, includes everything) — best for tech startups
2. Or [Clerky](https://www.clerky.com/) ($799) — designed for startups
3. Or DIY through your state's Secretary of State ($50-$300)

**After formation:**
- Get an EIN (free from IRS)
- Open a business bank account
- ASSIGN all IP to the company (critical — the company owns everything, not you personally)
- File an IP Assignment Agreement (transfers your personal IP to the company)

### STEP 7: Terms of Service & Privacy Policy (Before Launch)

**You need:**
1. **Terms of Service** — Protects you from user lawsuits, defines acceptable use
2. **Privacy Policy** — Required by law (GDPR, CCPA), defines data handling
3. **EULA (End User License Agreement)** — Defines what users can/can't do with your app
4. **Content License** — Defines who owns user-generated content (you need rights to use it)
5. **Creator Agreement** — If you have a creator marketplace, defines revenue split and IP ownership

**Options:**
- Use [Termly](https://termly.io/) or [TermsFeed](https://www.termsfeed.com/) for auto-generated versions ($15-$50/month)
- Hire a startup attorney for custom versions ($2,000-$5,000)
- At minimum, use auto-generated versions for launch, upgrade to custom later

### STEP 8: Investor/Partner Protection (Before Pitching)

**Before showing ANYONE your idea:**

1. **Create a pitch deck NDA** — Simplified, 1-page NDA for investor meetings
   - Note: Many VCs won't sign NDAs. In that case:
   - Only share high-level vision, not technical implementation details
   - Never share your code, algorithms, or data pipeline specifics
   - Share WHAT you do, not HOW you do it

2. **Document your prior art:**
   - Keep a dated invention notebook (physical or digital)
   - Screenshot everything with timestamps
   - Use git commits as dated proof of creation
   - Email yourself key innovations (creates dated record)

3. **Vesting schedules for co-founders:**
   - 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff (standard)
   - If a co-founder leaves early, they don't walk away with half the company
   - Use a Founders' Agreement (Clerky or attorney)

### STEP 9: Open Source vs. Proprietary Strategy

**What to keep proprietary (NEVER open source):**
- AI teacher personality engine
- Song translation pipeline
- Slang detection and verification system
- Voice cloning integration
- Recommendation algorithms
- User data and analytics
- Business logic and revenue systems

**What could be open source (for community/marketing):**
- Generic UI components (builds developer community)
- Language data contributions (community can add slang entries)
- Basic API wrappers (encourages ecosystem)
- Documentation and learning resources

**Rule of thumb:** If it's your competitive advantage, it stays proprietary. Period.

## What Happens If Someone Copies You

### If a competitor copies your feature:

1. **Document everything** — Screenshots, dates, evidence they saw your product first
2. **Send a cease and desist letter** — Attorney sends formal letter demanding they stop ($500-$1,000)
3. **File DMCA takedown** — If they copied code/content, file with their hosting provider
4. **Patent infringement claim** — If you have a patent, this is your nuclear option
5. **Trademark infringement** — If they use your name/logo, this is straightforward to win

### If an employee/contractor steals your code:

1. **Work-for-hire agreement** means you own it — they have no rights
2. **NDA violation** — Sue for breach of contract + damages
3. **Trade secret theft** — Federal crime under the Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA)
4. **Get an injunction** — Court order forcing them to stop immediately

### If an investor shares your idea:

1. **If they signed NDA** — Sue for breach
2. **If they didn't sign NDA** — This is why you only share WHAT, not HOW
3. **Your patents protect the HOW** — Even if they know the idea, they can't use your method
4. **First-mover advantage** — Execution matters more than ideas. Ship fast.
